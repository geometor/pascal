=====================================
Essay on Conics (English Translation)
=====================================

.. rubric:: Translated by Dr. Frances Marguerite Clarke (1929) · From the Paris Broadside (1640)

.. container:: title-page

   | **ESSAY ON CONICS**
   | **By B. P. [Blaise Pascal]**
   | *At Paris, M. DC. XL. (1640)*

----

Definitions
-----------

Definition I
~~~~~~~~~~~~

When several straight lines meet at the same point, or are parallel to each other, all these lines are said to be of the same order or of the same *ordonnance*, and the totality of these lines is termed an order of lines, or an *ordonnance* of lines.

Definition II
~~~~~~~~~~~~~

By the expression *conic section*, we mean the circumference of a circle, the ellipse, the hyperbola, the parabola, and an angle; since a cone cut parallel to its base, or through its vertex, or in the three other directions which produce respectively an ellipse, a hyperbola, and a parabola, produces in the conic surface either the circumference of a circle, or an angle, or an ellipse, a hyperbola, or a parabola.

Definition III
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

By the word *straight* used alone, we mean a straight line.

----

Foundational Lemmas
-------------------

Lemma I
~~~~~~~

If in the plane :math:`MSQ`, from the point :math:`M` proceed two straight lines :math:`MK, MV`, and from the point :math:`S` proceed two straight lines :math:`SK, SV`; and :math:`K` is the intersection of lines :math:`MK, SK`, and :math:`V` the intersection of lines :math:`MV, SV`, and :math:`A` the intersection of lines :math:`MK, SV`, and :math:`\mu` the intersection of lines :math:`MV, SK`; and through two of the four points :math:`A, K, \mu, V` which are not in the same straight line with points :math:`M, S`—as through points :math:`K, V`—passes a conic section intersecting the lines :math:`MV, MP, SV, SK` in points :math:`P, O, N, T`:

Then the lines :math:`MS, NO, TP` are of the same order.

Lemma II
~~~~~~~~

If through the same straight line pass several planes which are intersected by another plane, all the lines of intersection of these planes are of the same order with the line through which the said planes pass.

Lemma III (The Mystic Hexagram / Pascal's Theorem)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

If in the plane :math:`MSQ` on a conic section are chosen any six points :math:`P, K, V, O, T, N`, and lines are drawn joining them in succession forming a cyclic hexagon:

The intersections of opposite sides—namely the intersection of the first and fourth sides, that of the second and fifth sides, and that of the third and sixth sides—all lie in a single straight line: the **Pascal Line**.

----

Conclusion & Acknowledgments
----------------------------

We have several other problems and theorems, and several consequences deducible from the preceding; but the mistrust which I have of my slight experience and capacity does not permit me to advance more till my present effort has passed the examination of able men who may oblige me by looking at it.

We will also demonstrate this property, whose first inventor is **M. Desargues of Lyon**, one of the great spirits of this time, and of those most versed in mathematics, and among others in conics, whose writings on this subject, though few in number, have given ample testimony to those who have wished to receive understanding of it: and I am very willing to confess that I owe the little that I have found on this matter to the examples of his writings, and that I have tried to imitate, as far as possible, his method on this subject, which treats of conic sections without using the cone.

After that, if anyone thinks the subject worth continuing, I shall endeavor to extend it as far as God gives me the strength.
