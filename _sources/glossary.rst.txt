========
Glossary
========

Technical lexicon of early modern French projective terminology, classical Greek/Latin conic designations, and nineteenth-century incidence geometry.

.. glossary::

   ordonnance de lignes
   ordonnance
      A pencil or family of straight lines that either concur at a single finite point or are mutually parallel (concurrent at an ideal point at infinity). Introduced by Girard Desargues in his 1639 *Brouillon project* and adopted by Blaise Pascal in Definition I of the *Essay pour les coniques*.

   hexagramme mystique
   hexagrammum mysticum
      The "Mystic Hexagram" named by Blaise Pascal: the complete configuration formed by inscribing six points on an irreducible conic section and tracing the cyclic hexagons connecting them. Yields sixty Pascal lines, twenty Steiner points, sixty Kirkman points, and fifteen Plücker lines.

   droite de pascal
   pascal line
      The straight line passing through the three points of intersection of opposite sides of a hexagon inscribed in a conic section.

   point de steiner
   steiner point
      One of twenty points in the *Hexagrammum Mysticum* web at which three Pascal lines concur, discovered by Jakob Steiner in 1828. Arises by partitioning the six conic vertices into two complementary triangles $\triangle_A$ and $\triangle_B$ and permuting the vertices by the alternating group $A_3$ or coset $S_3 \setminus A_3$.

   point de kirkman
   kirkman point
      One of sixty points in the *Hexagrammum Mysticum* web where three Pascal lines concur in a second, distinct configuration discovered by Thomas Penyngton Kirkman in 1850. Three Kirkman points lie on each Pascal line.

   droite de plücker
   plücker line
      One of fifteen straight lines in the *Hexagrammum Mysticum* web, each carrying exactly four of the twenty Steiner points, discovered by Julius Plücker in 1830.

   birapport
   cross-ratio
      The fundamental projective invariant of four collinear points $(A, B; C, D) = \frac{AC \cdot BD}{CB \cdot DA}$, or four concurrent rays, invariant under central projection, perspectivities, and central projections from any point on a conic (Michel Chasles, 1865).

   section de cône
   conic section
      A curve formed by intersecting a circular cone by a plane: circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, or degenerate line-pair. In projective geometry $\mathbb{P}^2$, all non-degenerate conics are projectively equivalent to the circle.
